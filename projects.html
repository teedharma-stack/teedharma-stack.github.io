Thai IPO Performance Study (2025-26)

The question: if you had bought every Thai IPO of the last two years, would you have beaten simply buying the market?

I built an original dataset of all 19 companies that listed on the SET and mai in 2025-26, and measured each one's return since listing against the SET Index over the identical window, isolating the stock's own performance from the market's move.

Findings:

  • Median return since listing: -23% (63% now trade below their offer price)
  • Median return versus the market: -51 percentage points
  • Only 21% (4 of 19) beat simply holding the index

What makes it stark: the SET rose 20-44% over these windows, so IPO buyers lost ground while the broad market gained.

Method & limitations: self-collected data benchmarked per listing date; a small, recent sample (n=19) over a single window; simple, non-dividend-adjusted prices. Directionally robust; magnitudes would tighten with a longer history.

GameStop's Bid for eBay

View: the offer screens as roughly 65% accretive to GameStop's EPS, but that accretion is manufactured almost entirely by $2.0B of promised, unrealized synergies. Strip them out and it turns ~29% dilutive, while levering the combined company to about 9x EBITDA at close. Skeptical.

The math: ~$56B deal, 50% cash and 50% stock, ~1.26B new shares issued (nearly three times GameStop's base). Pro-forma EPS ~$1.54 versus $0.93 standalone; breakeven near $620M of synergies, about 31% of the $2.0B claimed.

Leverage: ~$30.6B pro-forma debt against ~$3.3B EBITDA is ~9x at close, above the ~5x prudence ceiling.

Sea Limited (SE): DCF Valuation

View: Sea screens as modestly undervalued. My DCF implies a value of about $136 per share versus roughly $108 in the market, around 26% upside, and it looks cheap across most reasonable assumptions. Recommendation: Buy.

The thesis: a real profitability inflection (FY2025 net income ~$1.6B, roughly triple the prior year, on revenue up 36% to $22.9B), growth that re-accelerated to +47% in Q1 2026, and a long runway as Southeast Asia's digital leader.

The valuation: seven years of free cash flow discounted at 11.5% with 3.5% terminal growth. Flexing those gives a range of roughly $91 to $177; the stock only screens fully valued above a ~13.5% discount rate.

Risks: competition (TikTok Shop, Lazada), the fintech loan book, Free Fire concentration, and a valuation where ~71% of value sits in the terminal year.

Project Foam: An LBO of Crocs

View: I would take Crocs private at roughly 9x EBITDA. It is a business that throws off enormous cash (about $625M of free cash flow on $4B of revenue, a 25% operating margin) and trades at a modest multiple because the market keeps betting the brand is a fad. Base case: a 15% IRR and a 2.0x return over five years.

The deal: take-private at 9x EBITDA (~$9B EV), funded with 5x leverage ($5B debt, $4B equity). Over five years EBITDA grows a modest 3% a year and all free cash flow sweeps into debt paydown.

Why it works: debt falls from $5B to about $2.2B (~$2.8B repaid), and exiting at the same 9x, equity grows from $4B to ~$8.2B. Almost all of the return comes from paying down debt with Crocs' own cash, not from the multiple going up.

The catch: Crocs is effectively a single-product brand with real fad risk. Leverage cuts both ways: if sales roll over, the same debt that drives the return drives a large loss. Do the deal only at a disciplined entry, not by chasing the 2026 rally.

Sea Limited: A Comps Check on the DCF

View: I valued Sea a second way, on trading comparables, to test the DCF. They do not fully agree, and that is the useful part. My DCF implied ~$136. Comps are more sobering: against the full peer set Sea already trades at a premium, and the peer median implies only about $87 (below the $108 market). Only against its closest premium comps (MercadoLibre, Coupang) does it imply about $115.

The peer set splits in two: premium growth names (MELI ~25x EBITDA, ~50x earnings) and cheap China-discount names (single-digit to low-teens EBITDA, ~10-12x earnings). Sea sits at the premium end, ~18x EBITDA and ~41x earnings, above the peer median of 13x and 37x.

Reconciling with the DCF: the DCF rewards Sea for future growth and its swing to profit, which trailing multiples cannot see. The two agree only if Sea keeps earning premium, MELI-like multiples, a bet on execution rather than a margin of safety.

Bottom line: on absolute cash flows Sea looks cheap; on relative multiples it looks fully valued against the broad group. A good business with real upside if it executes, not a deep-value bargain.

Kimberly-Clark's $48.7B Bid for Kenvue

View: This is the kind of deal GameStop's bid for eBay was not: strategically logical, prudently financed, and genuinely accretive. On an adjusted-earnings basis it lifts KMB's adjusted EPS by about 30%, and it needs only ~12% of the promised $2.1B synergies just to break even, so the margin of safety is real. Leverage lands near 3x. And yet KMB stock fell ~12% on the news. The reason is the price.

The deal: ~$48.7B for Kenvue (Tylenol, Band-Aid), $3.50 cash + 0.14625 KMB shares (~$21.01, a 46% premium). KMB issues ~280M new shares on a ~330M base, so Kenvue holders end up owning ~46% of the combined company. Close to a merger of equals.

The catch: the 30% is adjusted; on a GAAP basis, amortization of the acquired intangibles makes it dilutive for years. And a 46% premium for a litigation-clouded target, paid largely in stock, is a lot. The market's 12% haircut to KMB says the buyer is taking the risk.

Bottom line: a sound, accretive, prudently-levered deal that may simply have cost too much.
