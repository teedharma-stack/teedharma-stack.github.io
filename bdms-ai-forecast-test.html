Why test an AI forecast at all

01

AI gets numbers wrong

Finance teams can't use AI research unless they can check it. This workflow traces every number to its source, logs every error, and measures accuracy against reality.

02

Small companies have no analysts

Many smaller SET-listed companies have little or no broker coverage, so investors have no forecasts to work from. A process that is proven accurate could fill that gap.

03

BDMS is the test bench

BDMS is covered by many analysts, so their forecasts give a fair benchmark. If the process holds up here, it can be used where no benchmark exists.

How the test was kept fair

Every rule was set before results came out, and the forecast was made by an AI that could not see analysts' numbers. The AI did the data gathering and calculations; I designed the test, checked the data, reviewed the assumptions and interpreted the result.

What the checks caught

Catching errors is the point of the workflow. These were found and logged before the forecast was made.

  • 1
    An AI-written summary websitegave BDMS's 2025 occupancy as 68%. The company reports 64%. Source rejected.
  • 2
    The company's own quartersadd up to 40mm more than its full-year hospital revenue. The reported 3Q25 figure (27,356) was used.
  • 3
    The AI's own commentarysaid dividends weren't covered by cash flow before its formula had run. The real cover was 1.1x. Corrected and logged.
  • 4
    A bias risk in the designthe main chat had already seen a broker forecast, so the forecast was moved to a sealed chat that never saw it.

Why core profit missed: tax, not the business

Revenue and EBITDA were almost exact. I chose to keep a 25.5% EBITDA margin assumption against a falling trend; the actual came in at about 25.4%. The gap opened below EBITDA.

The forecast assumed an 18% tax rate. The actual rate was about 20%, because a one-off investment-board (BOI) tax incentive in 3Q25 did not repeat. Profit belonging to minority shareholders was also higher than assumed (about 123 vs 68).

Pre-tax profit, tax and minority figures for 3Q26 are from InnovestX's results note; minorities are derived as profit after tax minus net profit.

The verdict, under a rule set in advance

Each metric's error is |forecast − actual| ÷ actual. The errors are combined with more weight on core profit, the number the market watches. The AI passes if its weighted error is no more than 1 percentage point worse than the analysts'.

Weighted error =0.25 × revenue error + 0.5 × core profit error0.75

EBITDA (weight 0.25) is left out because no analyst published an EBITDA forecast; the remaining weights are rescaled. Rule fixed on 26 Oct 2026, before results.

Passed: pilot approved, provisionally. One quarter is a good sign, not proof. A final verdict needs four quarters. On core profit alone, the AI beat one broker, InnovestX (3.33% vs 3.62% error), but not the three-broker average (2.14%).

What's next

01

Tax and minorities added to the data

The forecasting data now includes tax incentives and the trend in minority interests, the two causes of this quarter's miss.

02

Keep scoring each quarter

Test #2, the BDMS 4Q26 forecast, locks on 9 Feb 2027, with results expected about 24–26 Feb 2027. Four quarters give a real verdict.

03

Pilot a company analysts skip

Nonthavej Hospital (SET: NTV) has no analyst coverage. Its forecast locks the same day, is scored against actual results only, and gets a valuation (DCF + comparables).
